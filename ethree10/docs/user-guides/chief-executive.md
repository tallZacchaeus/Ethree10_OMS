# Chief Executive — user guide

You are the overall head of Ethree10. Both branches — **Digital Media** and
**Tech & Product** — report to you.

## What you do here

Two things, and deliberately only two:

1. **See everything.** Every request, project, task, report and audit entry across
   both branches is visible to you.
2. **Approve the money.** You are the only role that can approve a project budget.
   Nothing can be invoiced to a client and no money can be spent until you do.

## What you deliberately cannot do

You cannot route requests, assign tasks, create projects, or approve delivery
work. That is not an oversight — each branch head knows their own people, their
skills and their current load far better than the top of the org does. Your voice
on delivery is the **note**: you can comment on any request, project or task, and
the team will see it.

You also cannot confirm that a payment was received. Approving the money and
confirming the money moved must be two different people — that is what makes the
approval chain worth anything in an audit. See *Separation of duties* below.

## Your screens

| Screen | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | A read-only pulse of the whole agency — queue health, throughput, capacity, delivery pressure. This is your home. |
| **Budget Approvals** | Your action surface. Everything awaiting your decision, plus your full decision history. |
| **Requests / Projects** | Read-through into any piece of work, with commenting. |
| **Reports** | Weekly and monthly rollups across both branches. |
| **Analytics** | Throughput over time and bottleneck detection. |
| **Audit** | The append-only record of every state change. |
| **Clients / People / Branches** | Who the agency serves and who works here. |

## Approving a budget

1. A branch head submits a budget for a project. You get a notification.
2. Open **Budget Approvals**. You see the project, the client, the branch, the
   amount, who submitted it, and any note they attached.
3. Add your own note — it is recorded permanently and shown to the submitter.
4. **Approve** or **Reject**.

Approving unlocks two things for that project: Finance can issue and send its
invoice, and the branch can request spend against it.

Rejecting sends it back. The branch can revise and resubmit.

**A revision always clears your approval.** If a branch raises the amount on an
already-approved budget, the budget returns to *awaiting approval* and comes back
to you. Nobody can quietly raise a ceiling you already signed off.

## Separation of duties

The system refuses to give one person both **Chief Executive** and **Finance
Manager**. It also refuses, per transaction, to let you confirm payment on a
budget you approved. If you try, you get a clear message explaining why rather
than a silent failure.

This is the control that makes the money trail defensible. Do not work around it
by sharing accounts.
